Real work / Quality governance / TELUS Digital

One view. Different decisions.

A leadership summary should connect quality findings to action without treating every channel or metric as the same problem.

Context & my contribution

Turning a reporting pack into a useful weekly read.

The problem

Sales, customer service and business-support work have different measures. A single blended quality score can hide the reason behind a miss or imply that a customer outcome is entirely within an agent's control.

My role

In my quality leadership role at TELUS Digital, I consolidated and communicated a weekly health-card summary across these workstreams. My sent stakeholder update explains the direction of movement, the recurring quality themes and the areas needing attention.

This was part of an ongoing team reporting process, not a solo analytics product.

Approach

Keep the meaning of each measure intact.

Read the channels separately

I compared current and prior-week performance within the relevant channel, then called out where trends moved in different directions. Improvement in one area did not cancel a risk elsewhere.

Separate performance from diagnosis

The report distinguishes KPI execution opportunities, scored quality defects and root-cause findings. Customer dissatisfaction and repeat-contact analysis need a different denominator from the overall audit score.

Make the next conversation clear

I highlighted resolution accuracy, expectation setting, communication and compliance themes rather than presenting a wall of scores. This gave the stakeholder review a focused set of discussion points.

Workflow: channel view → comparable period → count and rate checks → controllable/process split → leadership summary.
What the evidence supports

A clearer reporting output, not an invented impact claim.

The documented output is a health-card report and my stakeholder summary. The underlying work shows multiple performance directions and different root-cause categories. It does not establish that the summary itself caused a rise in business performance.

A follow-up should check whether the agreed actions were completed and whether the next comparable audit sample changed. That is the measurement plan, not a result claimed here.
