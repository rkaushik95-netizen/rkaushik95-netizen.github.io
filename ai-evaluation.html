The missed defect
deserves its own measure.

The fictional system flags 24 of 30 reference defects (80.0%). It misses 6 of 30 (20.0%). It also flags 8 of 70 reference-clear cases (11.4%) unnecessarily.

Whether either error is acceptable depends on severity and the cost of review. The sample cannot supply a deployment decision.

What follows

Review disagreements.
Then question the reference.

A proposed review workflow

  1. Inspect the 14 disagreements, with case evidence and label rules.
  2. Check whether the reference itself needs correction.
  3. Separate serious misses from low-risk differences.
  4. Repeat on a representative held-out sample before making a deployment decision.

Limits

These labels contain no underlying conversations or feature data. There is no trained system, random sample, uncertainty estimate or real error discovery. The study illustrates the math and a review workflow only.

An operational evaluation needs representative data, independent adjudication, severity rules, reviewer agreement and a clear decision threshold.

Explain precision, recall and agreement

Precision asks: of the 32 flagged defects, how many match the reference? 24 / 32 = 75.0%. Recall asks: of the 30 reference defects, how many were found? 24 / 30 = 80.0%. Agreement counts matching labels across all cases: (24 + 62) / 100 = 86.0%.
