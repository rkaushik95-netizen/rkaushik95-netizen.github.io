Real work / Audit quality / TELUS Digital

Check the report. Then trust the story.

Quality governance also applies to the people and reports measuring quality. An incorrect rate or an unrecorded decision can distort the next action.

Two documented contributions

Small controls with clear ownership.

Report validation

During review of a weekly audit update, I spotted an implausible defect-rate value and asked for the report to be proofread before distribution. The sent message records the issue and the requested correction.

I do not claim that every field was fixed or that the correction produced a business result. The evidence supports the review intervention.

Calibration follow-through

After a calibration discussion, I asked that its decisions also be recorded in the calibration tracker. The aim was to keep the shared reference aligned with the meeting rather than leaving the judgment in an email thread.

The evidence supports my follow-up request, not a claim that I designed the entire calibration process.

A practical control pattern

Three checks before a rate becomes a decision.

Meaning

What does the rate count: failed audits, individual parameter defects or root-cause classifications? These are different measures.

Arithmetic

Check numerator, denominator, percentage formatting and period. A percentage should be interpreted within the definition of the metric, not accepted because the cell is formatted correctly.

Traceability

Record calibration decisions where the team can find the current standard. An unresolved disagreement should stay visible until the evidence and rule are settled.

The checks above describe my operating approach. They are not a claim that every step was implemented in the two documented examples.
What changed

An issue surfaced. A follow-up requested.

My direct contribution was challenging a reporting error and requesting calibration traceability. Neither thread is used as proof of a measured reduction in errors or improved evaluator agreement.
