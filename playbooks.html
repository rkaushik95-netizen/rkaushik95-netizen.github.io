Real work / Playbooks & documentation / TELUS Digital

Put the way of working in writing.

A program that runs on tribal knowledge breaks when one person is away. Written playbooks turn habits into a system a new leader, trainer or analyst can pick up on day one.

Context & my contribution

Three manuals, one way of working.

The problem

A support program's real operating manual usually lives in chat history and in the memory of a few senior people. New team leaders, trainers and quality analysts learn the job by interrupting them, and expectations drift between teams because nothing is written down.

My role

In my quality leadership role at TELUS Digital, I created and drafted a three-part playbook set for one program: an operational playbook for team leaders, a training delivery schedule for new-hire batches, and a quality playbook for analysts and the QA lead. Each lays out daily, weekly and monthly tasks with named ownership, execution detail and the tracker where the work is recorded.

The documents live in the program's shared drive under my account. The quality playbook was reviewed and refined with the QA manager, and I organized the closing review of the set with program leadership.

Approach

Write for the person who starts on Monday.

One place, not thirty answers

Each playbook is built as a single reference: what to do daily, weekly and monthly, who owns it, and where it is recorded. A question about how the work runs should have exactly one place to go.

Execution detail, not principles

The audience is the new team leader, the trainer's first batch, the analyst on their first audit. Tasks are written as concrete steps with owners and cadences, so the document can be followed without a translator.

Review it like a process change

The drafts went through stakeholder comments and a closing review with leadership, the same way a process change would. A playbook has to carry agreement, not just authorship, or nobody follows it.

Structure: daily / weekly / monthly tasks, each with a named owner, execution detail and the tracker where it is recorded.
What the evidence supports

An authored, reviewed set, described at the level the evidence reaches.

The documented output is three playbook files created from my account within one week, and the review trail around them: revision history shows me as the quality playbook's creator with later revisions from the reviewing QA manager, document comments show stakeholder review, and the final playbook review meeting was organized by me with program leadership. The evidence supports that I authored the set and drove its review.

It does not claim the playbooks moved a business metric; adoption is the work that comes next, and a fair check would be whether new joiners ramp on the documents rather than on interruptions. It also does not claim sole credit: reviewers shaped the final versions, and that is the point of a playbook.
