02 / Retail analytics / Public data

Growth needs a
clean denominator.

An independent analysis of the UCI Online Retail dataset. The question: what do complete-month sales lines show after duplicate and cancellation checks?

541,909 Original transaction lines
5,268 Exact duplicates · 1.0% of original rows
524,878 Positive sale lines after cleaning
The finding

November is highest.
December is not comparable.

November 2011 has £1,503,866.78 of positive sales-line value across 82,004 lines. That is £352,603.05 (30.6%) above October's £1,151,263.73 across 58,537 lines.

The dataset ends on 9 December 2011. Comparing that partial month to November would create a misleading decline. The chart therefore uses January to November 2011 only.

The UK contributes £7,664,042.13 of £9,182,867.74 (83.5%) of positive sales-line value in Jan-Nov 2011, across 417,931 of 459,054 lines (91.0%). Check country concentration before using the total as a broad market story.
Cleaning rules

Count the exclusions.
Keep the meaning intact.

From raw lines to the chart

  1. Read the original Excel file and preserve invoice identifiers as strings.
  2. Drop 5,268 exact duplicate rows, leaving 536,641 unique lines.
  3. Exclude invoices beginning with C, non-positive quantity and non-positive price from the positive-sales view.
  4. That removes 11,763 unique lines (2.19%). Keep 524,878 positive sale lines.
  5. Compute Quantity × UnitPrice in GBP and aggregate complete months.

What the result means

Sales-line value is not accounting revenue. Cancelled transactions are excluded from this view rather than netted against sales. The dataset has no product cost, so it cannot support profit or margin claims.

135,037 of 536,641 unique lines (25.2%) have no customer ID. They remain in aggregate sales analysis; customer segmentation would need a separate rule.

An exact duplicate is a cleaning assumption, not proof of a source-system mistake. Compare with a retained-duplicate view before using this for a business decision.

How to explain this in a conversation

"I checked row quality before the trend. I separated cancelled and positive sales lines, then compared only complete months. November is the highest observed month. I would not call that causal growth or forecast next year from one year of data."

The next checks would be product mix, repeat customers with known IDs, cancellation patterns and sensitivity to deduplication.
