Where should
coaching start?

A fictional audit dataset shows why an overall pass rate is not enough to set a coaching plan.

All 1,200 audit records are generated for this exercise. No employer, client or employee data is used. Results illustrate a method, not real performance.

The decision

Start with resolution.
Keep process separate.

Resolution is the largest defect category: 132 of 346 failures (38.2%). It is a sensible first coaching hypothesis. Process defects account for 85 of 346 failures (24.6%) and need a different response.

No improvement claim is made. Test the coaching action, monitor the next sample and check whether the defect rate changes.

Method and limits

The denominator changes the story.

What the study does

  1. Generate 1,200 records with random seed 41.
  2. Assign one outcome per record: pass or one primary defect.
  3. Calculate failures per audit and defect mix per failed audit separately.
  4. Label resolution, documentation and communication as coachable for this exercise only.

The data contains no people, interaction transcripts or dates. This is deliberately a minimal teaching model.

What it cannot prove

Synthetic patterns are designed, not discovered in a real operation. Audit results also reflect sample design. These numbers cannot establish a team's true defect rate or that coaching caused a change.

A production study would need a defined sampling plan, calibrated auditors, severity, multi-defect handling and a follow-up period.
