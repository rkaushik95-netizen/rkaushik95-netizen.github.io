Readiness needs evidence.

Training completion is only part of the handover. A new quality analyst also needs to apply the standard consistently on real work.

Context & ownership

From training plan to operational handover.

The problem

A new QA was moving through training, shadowing, practical audits and nesting. Tool access was still a dependency. The handover needed a clear distinction between what had been taught, what had been practised and what had been checked.

My contribution

I communicated progress, completed learning areas, the access blocker and the next readiness stage to stakeholders. I later communicated the final readiness result and transition into regular QA responsibilities.

A colleague performed the independent audit-the-auditor review. I did not claim that review as my own work.

Approach

Make the handover stages visible.

Build the foundation

The documented plan covered program context, process standards, tools, report exports, case-handling practice, side-by-side observation and dedicated questions.

Track dependencies

I made the outstanding tool permission visible instead of describing the handover as complete while a required access step was still open.

Check judgment independently

An audit-the-auditor exercise compared the new QA's parameter judgments with the review standard. It covered acknowledgment, communication, resolution, response timelines and status handling.

Close the transition

My final update recorded that the readiness benchmark was met and that the QA had moved into routine audits, briefings, calibration and operational meetings.

Observed result

A completed readiness handover.

The sent update documents successful completion of the readiness check and transition to regular QA responsibilities. This is an internal readiness assessment, not a professional certification awarded to me or the analyst.

No claim is made about long-term productivity, reduced defects or faster onboarding. Those would need a follow-up study with a defined baseline and comparable samples.

Confidentiality & evidence note

Based on my September 2026 sent onboarding progress and final handover updates. Individual names, internal tools, client context, accuracy rates, audit counts and private documents are omitted. My coordination and communication are separated from the colleague's independent review.
